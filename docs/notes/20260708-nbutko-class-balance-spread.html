Class Balance Spread

The Wizard is structurally the weakest class and the Rogue the strongest, even after the M4/M5 combat retune closed most of the gap — measured, not guessed, by the sim harness.

author: nbutko · opened: 2026-07-08 · relates to: combat-retune PRD
When to read this. Read this if you're touching class balance, ability magnitudes (config/abilities.ts), the weapon ladder (config/weapons.ts), or wondering why the Wizard feels harder to play than the other three classes. The gap is measured and structural, not a bug: a d6 hit die plus a wand line that (even after the retune) still starts a level behind the other classes' first weapon upgrade. It survived a whole retune pass aimed at exactly this kind of spread, so don't expect a small tweak to close it — and don't assume it's already fixed just because a later story mentions "the wand line."

Relevant when

You're retuning class/ability/weapon numbers, or need to know which class to pick for a "does this feel fair" playtest — the Wizard is the stress case, the Rogue the easy case.

The gotcha

The pre-retune sim (the combat-retune PRD's §7, Finding 3) measured on-track win rates of 25–90% for the Wizard against 88–100% for Fighter/Rogue/Bard at the same (level, wpm) — a single flat-die wand (d6) with no upgrade path, against Rogue's sneak-attack burst winning most one-shot races.

Story 3 gave the Wizard a wand-line (+1/+2/+3 INT weapons) and wired the dead crit subsystem, and the Wizard's on-track win rate climbed sharply (e.g. Dungeon 9, 38% → 68%). It is not closed, though — Story 3's own "flagged for user verification" note and the final converged surface in the PRD's §9 both still show the Wizard (W column) as the lowest on-track win rate of the four classes at almost every boss: D1 8% vs. 83/83/90 for the others, D6 40% vs. 93/100/100, D8 30% vs. 57/100/100, D9 70% vs. 98/100/100, D10 30% vs. 90/100/100 (D11 is saturated at 100% for every class). The Rogue is at or near 100% in every one of those rows — the strongest class on the converged surface, same as it was pre-retune.

The cause is structural, not a missed knob: the Wizard has the lowest hit die (d6, vs. d8–d10 for the others) so it has the least HP margin, and its Arcane Mind class feature (crits roll 3 dice instead of 2) rewards landing big hits without doing anything to help it survive long enough to land them. Fighter also trails Rogue/Bard by more than Finding 3's original framing suggested — the retune closed the worst of the gap but did not equalize the four classes.

What to do

Don't re-derive this from first principles — it's already measured. Before touching class balance again, re-run the sweep and read the surface next to §9's "after" table rather than assuming Story 3's wand-line fully fixed it. The sim harness that produces these numbers lives in src/engine/sim/: balance.ts (simulateCharacterBattles, the per-battle model — class, level, abilities, weapon, buffs, encounter roll, served tier, real computeDamage) and balance.test.ts (unit coverage of the sim's own building blocks, e.g. the weak/strong hit-magnitude helper). The sweep script that runs it across every dungeon boss × corner × class and prints win rate / hits-to-kill / crit rate / dodge rate is content-pipeline/retune-sweep.ts (npx tsx content-pipeline/retune-sweep.ts). src/engine/sim/combat-invariants.ts is the related invariant suite (win-rate monotonicity, the hits-to-kill band) that gates the surface from regressing, not from closing this specific class gap.

If it's worth closing further: the Wizard's HP die and the Arcane Mind feature's total dependence on landing hits (rather than any survivability trade) are the two structural levers a future retune would need to pull — see Story 3's own "flagged for user verification" note before assuming this is settled.
